Understanding your costs

How to read a processing statement

A quoted rate is a starting point. Your statement helps show what you actually paid across a month of business.

Merchant Pro resource · 4 minute read

Start with a complete month.

Gather every page of a recent statement. If equipment, software, or other services are billed separately, keep those charges nearby too. Seasonal businesses may benefit from comparing a typical month with a busier period.

Find the volume and total fees.

Identify the payment volume and processing fees for the same period. Check which transactions are included and whether refunds, chargebacks, or other adjustments affect the numbers.

An illustrative calculation

If a statement shows $20,000 in processing volume and $600 in processing fees, the effective rate is:

$600 ÷ $20,000 × 100 = 3.00%

These are made-up numbers to explain the calculation, not a Merchant Pro quote, savings estimate, or benchmark.

This blended percentage helps describe that statement. It does not tell you which charges are negotiable or predict what a different month will cost.

Ask what makes up the total.

  • Which charges depend on transaction volume or transaction count?
  • Which are recurring monthly or annual charges?
  • Are equipment and software costs included or billed elsewhere?
  • Were there any unusual adjustments this month?

Compare the same things.

When reviewing a proposal, ask which fees it includes, what assumptions it uses, and which costs may be separate. Your card mix, average sale, and ways of accepting payments can affect the result.

Bring questions, not just numbers.

Note any confusing fees, reporting problems, equipment limitations, or support concerns. A useful review considers how the setup works for your business as well as what it costs.
